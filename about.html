Bilbo Data

LiveAbout

Bilbo Data measures how busy the streets of Crown Heights are — how many cars, trucks, buses, bikes, and pedestrians move through key intersections, and how that rises and falls through the day.

AGGREGATE ONLY

Bilbo Data counts how many, never who. It records totals by type — it does not identify, follow, or keep a record of any individual person or vehicle. Nothing here re-identifies a car or tracks anyone's movements.

Where the data comes from

Thirty public NYC DOT traffic cameras within a 2.5-mile circle centered on Kingston Avenue & President Street. These are the city's own low-resolution (352×240) traffic stills, published for public use.

How it works

  • Every minute, each camera is sampled repeatedly (~25 frames).
  • A computer-vision model counts objects by type in each frame.
  • The minute is averaged into a single reading — one minute is the finest measurement — and added to the running history.
  • The dashboard rolls that history into live numbers, windowed totals (last minute / hour / 24 h / 30 days), and charts.

How often it updates

Fresh data lands about every minute. Numbers reflect the average count in view during that minute, not a single snapshot.

How it runs

A scheduled cloud job does the sampling and counting; the results are stored as an open dataset and this page reads from it directly. No servers to keep on, no cost. Camera imagery isn't rebroadcast — only the counts derived from it.
